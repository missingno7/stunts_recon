# Canary declaration-order experiment (desktop host)

This is isolated research on blocked `is_facing_camera`, not a production reopen or matching claim. The pinned MSC 5.1 medium profile and MS-DOS Player runner were used. The exact tested source is `declaration-order-20260923.c` (SHA-256 `53b0dae261462d2a307e92c7bf41adc0f352219e1c60ef56f11ee45f9de8637c`); the controlled comparator is `prior_10/source.c`. The only source difference is the declaration order of `dx1` and `dy0`.

Question: does placing the X deltas before the Y deltas select the target's paired 32-bit BP homes? Before compiling, the worker predicted `dx1` would move to BP-0xC/BP-0xA and `dy0` to BP-8/BP-6 while the 0x10 frame and six-byte return/extent gap could remain. Unchanged homes would falsify the proposed allocation mechanism. The worker's hard ceiling was one meaningful iteration and one compiler process; one process ran.

The prediction held for the stack homes. A later research-only two-source batch recompiled both forms and found two unique effective outputs, identical object declarations and fixups, and 11 changed common byte positions. The candidate-vs-candidate diagnosis grouped those positions as one consistent BP displacement mapping, with no contradictions in the observed aligned scope. Compared with the old order, the paired order gained 35 target byte-anchor positions and lost none. Its target diagnosis had 75 exact aligned instructions (86.21% target anchored), versus 63 (72.41%) for the old order. These are diagnostic observations, not acceptance.

Both complete contributions remain 194 bytes against the pristine 200-byte target. The new form first diverges at the branch target at relative +0x4A. Result/return lowering remains unresolved, so no production attempt was spent and no source was promoted. The declaration-order result explains one local stack-allocation difference for this source and profile; it does not explain the six-byte extent gap or establish unique historical source.

Detailed prediction, compiler receipt/log and full diagnosis are local under `build/research/is_facing_camera_decl_order_20260923/`. The final batch tool check archived its sources, original objects, compiler logs, deduplicated outcomes and pair diagnosis under `build/private/research-batches/is_facing_camera/44fe373bead1/` on this desktop. Three tool checks used two compiler processes each; those six are separate from the worker's one-process original probe. The batch corroborates the original probe and adds a compact candidate comparison, not another recovery mechanism.

The source `ordered-cast-20260923.c` (SHA-256 `93a6a6d5b6bf013050023e7623681a5fcf0579d5e2b5d78194c9ce3634f17b1f`) combines the corrected declaration order with a `(char)` cast on the final Boolean result. The machine prediction, falsifier, one-iteration and one-compiler-process ceilings were frozen in `build/research/is_facing_camera_ordered_cast_20260923/plan.json` before its single compile.

The pinned compiler emitted a unique 198-byte complete contribution with the paired BP homes and an AL/CBW return path. The early zero-return topology and missing target trailing false epilogue/NOP sequence remain; the pristine 200-byte contribution is still unmatched. The target diagnosis counted 69 exact aligned instructions (79.31% target anchored) and eight mismatch islands. Thus the cast independently selects the byte-return mechanism but does not recover the placement or six-byte gap by itself. The exact source, object, log and diagnosis remain under `build/research/is_facing_camera_ordered_cast_20260923/`. This is research-only, with no grinder attempt or production promotion.
