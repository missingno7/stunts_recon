# Matched model-routing pilot

**Decision: retain Luna/high first, with evidence-driven escalation.** This pilot provides no observed Astra-only solve and no support for an Astra-first task class. Keep Astra available for a concrete unresolved question after tooling/evidence checks, rather than escalating solely because three source attempts failed. This is an engineering choice under the measured price gap, not proof of equal model capability.

All eight approved runs finished at high reasoning effort in fresh paired capsules. The four Luna/Astra pairs began with identical frozen input manifests. Four runs earned strict whole-image receipts on the two prequalified known-solvable cases; neither model earned a strict receipt on the two unresolved cases. Production source and acceptance manifests were untouched.

The two known-solvable replays support only a tiny observed order statistic: Astra succeeded on proposed trial 1 in both; Luna succeeded on trial 1 and trial 4. For the Luna rectangle run, the third proposal was blocked before compilation by the omitted optional `msc500` profile; the fourth proposal succeeded after compilation. Do not generalize this two-function curve into a repository-wide K estimate. All four strict outputs match identities in the indexed archive. That archive includes later records, so a match does not establish that the worker had already seen the output. The pilot recovered zero new production C bytes.

`parse_shape2d_helper` reported supported-explanation exhaustion at nine proposals per model. Luna `unknown_libname_1` reported supported-source-shape exhaustion at eleven; Astra reported `SEARCH_CONVERGED` at eight, which is a worker stop status rather than independently established exhaustion. None reached the 15-hypothesis or 20-compiler cap. Two Luna precompiler failures (rectangle helper trial 3 and parse helper trial 7) attempted a locked optional `msc500` profile that the phase-1 builder had omitted; the mismatch named `C1.ERR` in the sibling capsule and launched zero compiler processes. These are tooling-blocked attempts, not model failures. The unknown Astra terminal report also mentions a QuickC BAKPAT rejection; classify it as a tooling-blocked diagnostic attempt, with no extra invocation beyond the reconciled process/probe/receipt counts. The builder was corrected for future capsules; the missing-profile repair and any phase-2 model run remain unexecuted.

Five earlier launch preflights are separate from the eight completed runs: two CLI argument-validation failures, two network-transport failures, and one launcher `PermissionError`. They produced no request-token deltas. Every completed run's CLI `turn.completed` usage matches its session-log request totals for comparable fields. An eight-run integrity audit also reconciled state, per-trial, archived process, fresh-probe, and receipt counts; no direct compiler/acceptance command appeared outside the recorded helper. This includes the QuickC/BAKPAT mention in the unknown Astra log; no unexplained invocation was found. Effective-output identities are receipt-derived and do not prove physical-code uniqueness across flags or profiles. The detailed 592-request ledger, including root and preparation activity after the cutoff, is in [usage.json](usage.json). Reasoning output is included within output tokens.

Standard-equivalent direct cost across the eight runs was $5.21717 Astra and $0.08130442 Luna, a 64.17× observed ratio including the five zero-token preflights. These are alternative Standard/Fast pricing scenarios from the saved pricing reference, not an invoice. Official unit rates make Astra five times SOL and SOL twenty times Luna, but observed token volume and cache mix make the measured pilot ratio differ from that nominal 100× rate ratio. Root supervision added a $26.25143 Standard scenario; priced non-root preparation added $0.41454. Codex auto-review added 39 requests (1,829,923 input tokens, 1,598,720 cached and 4,293 output) that the pricing reference does not price, so total supervision cost is unknown.

Helper-trial time was 1.993–4.558 seconds per run while full wall time was 96–639 seconds. The remainder includes model reasoning, orchestration, startup, and other tool activity; it is not isolated model latency. Paired launches overlapped by 54.761 seconds for `copy_string`, 116.948 for `rect_compare_point`, 384.680 for `parse_shape2d_helper`, and 376.882 for `unknown_libname_1`. This confirms concurrency in the harness, not a causal throughput advantage. The short helper time only shows that compiler trials were cheap in this cohort; it does not show batching would help.

This study matched evidence and effort but did not ablate context, tools, or history, and did not transfer one model’s solution to another during phase 1. The four selected functions cannot establish that Astra should be the first-class default or represent the broader workload. If another model-cost test is wanted, use one preregistered unresolved function in identical fresh capsules with the corrected profile set and equal high-effort, hypothesis, compiler, and strict-acceptance limits for SOL and Luna. No SOL run was included here.

## Decision limits and stopping rule

- Existing proposal-only comparisons and the unmatched endurance cohort could not answer strict cost-per-solve; they were reused for eligibility and historical negatives rather than rerun as purported matched data. See [the initial audit](audit.md).
- Both models solved 2/4 selected tasks, all in the two known-solvable replays. Including unsuccessful tasks, direct Standard-equivalent cost per strict replay solve was about $2.609 for Astra versus $0.04065 for Luna (64.17×). This is not cost per new production recovery: none was produced.
- Across all four selected tasks, cumulative strict solves at one proposed hypothesis were Astra 2/4 and Luna 1/4; Luna reached 2/4 at four proposals, or three compiler-executed hypotheses. Neither model reached 80% or 90% of this four-task cohort. The median among the two solved cases is 1 versus 2.5 proposed hypotheses, or 1 versus 2 executed hypotheses; these tiny empirical summaries are not population estimates.
- Luna's rectangle result is a late success by proposal index, but not evidence of success after more than three executed hypotheses: the missing compiler consumed one proposal without testing it. No extended unresolved run produced a strict late win. This small sample neither validates a universal three-attempt cutoff nor proves that longer search is useless.
- Astra skipped some rectangle search steps, but the pilot found no unique successful mechanism requiring Astra. The same source-level solution was independently reached by Luna. No mechanism-transfer experiment or context-size ablation was performed.
- Keep production acceptance and its existing gates unchanged. Within authorized isolated research, continue while a new prediction can discriminate among supported hypotheses; after two equivalent outcomes change the analysis level. Stop on strict proof, demonstrated missing capability/evidence, convergence, or an explicit budget. Refund neither compiler events nor failed predictions silently; distinguish setup failures from tested negatives.
- Parallel independent workers can overlap model/tool latency. The experiment did not measure throughput scaling with worker count or isolate context processing, reasoning generation, service queueing and ordinary tool latency. Compiler trials already use MS-DOS Player; changing from DOSBox-X would not address the pilot's runtime.
- Supervision cost dwarfed the cheap workers. Repeated supervisor inspection, preparation, permission handling and reporting must be reduced before claiming an economical end-to-end recovery workflow. Preserve compact machine-readable handoffs and reuse the prepared tools; the experiment's setup cost is not evidence of normal per-function production cost.
